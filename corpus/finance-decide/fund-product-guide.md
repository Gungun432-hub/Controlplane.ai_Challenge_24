# Finance — Retail Fund Product Guide (v7.1, Q3 2026)

## Regulatory position
All statements about investment products made to a retail client are regulated
communications. Nothing in this guide may be presented as a personal
recommendation. Past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results
and every communication must say so.

## Horizon Balanced Fund
Objective: capital growth with moderate volatility over a 5 to 7 year horizon.
Target return: 8.4 per cent annualised, measured over rolling five year periods.

**The target is not a guarantee.** The fund has no capital protection, no
guaranteed return, and no floor. In the 2022 drawdown the fund fell 14.2 per cent
peak to trough over eleven months.

Asset allocation as at 30 June 2026: 58 per cent equities, 34 per cent fixed
income, 8 per cent cash and equivalents.
Ongoing charge: 0.82 per cent per annum. Exit charge: none after 12 months; 1 per
cent within the first 12 months.
Minimum investment: 25,000 rupees lump sum, or 2,500 rupees monthly.

## Horizon Income Fund
Objective: income distribution with capital preservation as a secondary aim.
Target distribution: 5.1 per cent annualised, paid quarterly.
The distribution is variable and has been reduced twice in the last decade, in
2020 and 2023. Ongoing charge: 0.64 per cent.

## Horizon Growth Fund
Objective: long term capital growth over 10 years or more. No income
distribution. Higher volatility: a fall of 25 per cent or more in any twelve
month period is within the expected range for this fund.
Ongoing charge: 0.95 per cent.

## Language that is never permitted
Do not use: guaranteed, assured, risk-free, no-risk, promised, locked-in,
certain, or safe, in connection with any return figure.
Do not quote a return figure without the period it is measured over.
Do not compare a fund's target to a bank deposit rate without stating that a
deposit is capital protected and a fund is not.

## Suitability
A retail client must be given the Key Information Document before any
application. A client under 12 months from a stated liquidity need should not be
directed to the Growth Fund. Any client who states they cannot tolerate a loss of
capital must be referred to a human adviser.
