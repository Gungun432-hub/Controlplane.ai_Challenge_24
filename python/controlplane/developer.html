01Integrate

One line changes. You keep your model, your prompts and your SDK.

ControlPlane sits in front of the tool call, not inside your prompt. You send what your agent proposes; you get back a decision and a receipt.

# before
client = OpenAI(base_url="https://api.your-provider.com/v1")

# after — the only line that changes
client = OpenAI(base_url="https://controlplane.internal/v1")

Or call the gate directly when your agent already chooses its own tools:

curl -X POST http://localhost:8000/api/turn \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -H 'X-ControlPlane-Token: $CONTROLPLANE_OPERATOR_TOKEN' \
  -H 'X-ControlPlane-Actor: your-service-name' \
  -d '{"systemId": "support-copilot",
       "message": "Draft a reply confirming the delivery window.",
       "toolHint": "ticketing"}'
What you may not send. Three fields are refused outright, because if you could supply them the governance would be decorative:
blastRadiusriskPrice action / routesources (as text) governanceStatus
Blast radius is derived from the tool binding, never declared. Sources are cited by ID and resolved server-side against an immutable corpus, so a model cannot author the evidence it is checked against.

05The adjudicator, and how we know it works

A second model checks the answers our documents cannot settle. The obvious objection is that you are now trusting a model to check a model — so it has a labelled test set in the repository, and a score.
  • Two charges, one call. Per claim: does this need a source, and does the source settle it. Whole answer: does it leave out a condition in the sources that changes what you should do. Two judgements, one model call.
  • Triggered, not routine. Only when the evidence is already weak and the price is already above the pass line — or when nothing any system holds covers the subject at all. A clean grounded answer never pays for a judge.
  • It cannot decide. Its output is an ordinary advisory signal, priced with everything else. It cannot lift a hard gate, lower the evidence floor or un-block an irreversible action.
  • It fails closed. Where the judgement was load-bearing and the judge could not be reached, the answer escalates. An unchecked answer is not a cleared answer.
  • A low-confidence accusation is downgraded. A contradicted verdict below 0.45 confidence becomes uncovered — we would rather say nobody can confirm it than call it wrong on a coin flip. The downgrade is recorded.

06Purpose satisfaction — the failure no model refuses

Every sentence true, nothing to flag, and the person still does not get what they came for. This runs on the answer, needs no documents, costs nothing, and it is the one check here you can break by typing.

The case this was built for, in our mentor's words:

“My car wash station is two kilometres from my house, but I'm a fitness freak and I want to burn 200 calories today. Should I walk or drive?” — “The answer was: since you're a fitness freak, I suggest you walk. Then what is the purpose of going to a car wash? The purpose is lost.”

He chose it deliberately over a violent example: “if you say I want to kill my employee, you go and check with Gemini, it will block you — their models are sophisticated. If you can think about something that the models will not block, think about it.” No frontier model refuses this. Nothing in the answer is false. Grounding passes it. A second model agrees with it. And the car stays dirty.

Why the off-topic check cannot see it. That check fires only when an answer shares no subject word with the question — and here walk, calories, kilometres and fitness all overlap. Measured across the whole answer this is perfectly on topic. It is.

So we measure one level down. A question does not have one topic; it has one or more objectives, and an answer can be entirely on topic while abandoning one of them. We pull the objectives out of the question — a goal the person states outright (“I want to burn 200 calories”) and an errand, the thing the trip is for (“to my car wash station”) — then check the answer engages each one. The errand is the one that gets dropped, because it is assumed rather than argued for, so it is the one we name.

6 abandoned objectives, 6 caught 20 ordinary answers, 0 false alarms 0.04 ms no model call

The false alarm is the expensive error here: telling somebody their perfectly good answer ignored them is the fastest way to get the whole control plane switched off. So an objective counts as served if the answer engages any of its distinctive words. Deliberately generous.

08The two supervisors, and who wins

We were asked where the supervisory agents are in our architecture, and who wins when they disagree. The honest answer is that both were already here and neither was named. This names them — and it is read from the same place the code reads it, so this page cannot describe an architecture the running system does not have.

loading…

Two of Safety's gates run before the model. The judgement there is Safety's — is this a request to harm somebody, is that a word this organisation will not send out — and the position is Cost's contribution: running them at the door is why a refused question costs nothing. Neither supervisor owns that pair alone, so a receipt for a pre-model refusal names both.

What we are not claiming. These are not autonomous LLM agents. They are deterministic supervisors with written charters, and there is no negotiation between them — arbitration is a fixed rule, because a conversation is a thing an attacker can join. The one model-based check we do run, the adjudicator in section 05, is explicitly advisory and cannot lift a gate.

Every receipt carries a supervisors block naming which supervisor raised what, who decided, and under which rule — so the attribution is auditable per decision rather than asserted on a slide.

09What we do not claim

Read this before you argue with a score.
  • Grounding is lexical support checking against the resolved corpus, plus numeric conflict detection. It is not semantic verification, and it will miss a paraphrase that shares no vocabulary with its source.
  • Privacy finds format-valid candidate identifiers — Luhn for 13–19 digit card numbers, Verhoeff for 12-digit national IDs. A random 17-digit number fails the checksum and is not flagged. This is deliberate: a detector that flags every long number is a detector nobody reads.
  • Fairness is a bounded counterfactual probe over four substitution pairs. It detects a flip on those probes. It is not a fairness guarantee.
  • Cost compares against a rolling median of the last 200 decisions. On a cold window it has no opinion and says so rather than guessing.
  • Completeness (the omission check) is pattern matching over the sentences retrieval surfaced. It finds a condition the answer walked past; it cannot judge whether walking past it mattered in your situation, and it will miss an omission phrased in words that appear nowhere in your documents. That case is what the adjudicator's second charge is for.
  • Subject scope is lexical. It is a routing aid, deliberately asymmetric — it would much rather answer a borderline question under full governance than turn a colleague away from the assistant that could have helped them.
  • The adjudicator is a language model. It is advisory by construction, scored against a labelled set that we wrote, and the set is small. We report the false alarm rate next to the catch rate because for a checker the false alarm is the expensive error.
  • Refusals are detected by pattern, not understood. An assistant that declines in wording we do not recognise will have its refusal measured as a claim; the failure direction is a false warning, never a missed one.
  • Capability overclaim is checked against the registry for the verbs we have mapped. An unmapped verb produces no finding at all rather than a guessed requirement — we would rather miss one than invent one.
  • Budgets. Reviewer capacity is a real cap with a reservation, so concurrent questions cannot each pass the same pre-flight check. Spend is a warning only, and we say why: a token cap that bit would need thousands of questions, and one that bit quickly would be a fiction.
  • Costs. Model spend uses one flat rate per thousand tokens, not a per-model input/output price, and the offline provider estimates tokens from text length. The reviewer-minute rate is an assumption we publish (₹8/min), not a measured figure. Both are labelled as estimates on every surface that shows them.
  • The operator token is a prototype boundary, not enterprise IAM. It is a shared secret, not authenticated identity, and the name on a review is attribution rather than proof. Reads that can return a person's words are behind it; aggregate and configuration reads are not.
  • There is no external tool executor in this build. The gate sits in front of the tool call and decides; nothing downstream of it actually issues a payment, a send or a restart. "Withheld" means the call was not issued by this system, and a production integration would need the executor to run behind the gate rather than beside it.
  • Seeded history is synthetic and labelled as such on the portfolio. Period figures separate it from live traffic.
  • The ledger is tamper-evident under protected key management — a SHA-256 chain with an HMAC signature. It is not tamper-proof, and we will not call it that. A tampered tail is detected before the next entry chains onto it and is reported by /health, but by default the log keeps writing: a governance system that stops recording the moment something looks wrong destroys the trail an investigator needs, and hands anyone who can touch one byte a way to silence it. LEDGER_STRICT_APPEND=1 switches to refusing instead.
